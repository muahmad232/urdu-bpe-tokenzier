1. Executive Summary & Architecture Overview

This report documents the design, implementation, and empirical analysis of an original Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) tokenizer built specifically for the Urdu language from an empty directory, without reliance on external tokenizer or NLP libraries. The project fulfills all CLO 3 requirements: collecting a diverse multi-genre Urdu corpus (>600 MB raw), establishing an idempotent Unicode normalization pipeline, implementing a 2-stage byte-level BPE tokenizer with deterministic tie-breaking and custom regex pre-tokenization, training to exactly 50,000 vocabulary tokens, and evaluating compression against industry-standard tokenizers.

Key Architectural Decisions
  • Base Vocab (0–255): 256 raw bytes ensuring 100% lossless UTF-8 roundtrips.
  • Special Token (256): <|endoftext|> isolated from merges.
  • Learned Merges (257–49,999): 49,743 merges via frequency-weighted inverted index.
  • Tie-Breaking: Lexicographically greater integer tuple (id1, id2).
  • Regex: Custom Urdu pattern preserving diacritics (\p{M}) & ZWNJ (\u200C).

2. Corpus Pipeline & Training Telemetry

Corpus Composition: Combining self-scraped journalism (52.76 MB raw from Daily Express, Daily Jang, and BBC Urdu across >2,200 documents) and public corpora (Makhzan classical literature 47.82 MB + streamed CC-100 502.58 MB). Total raw size reached 603.15 MB.

Cleaning & Splitting: The corpus was normalized via normalize.py, validated for Urdu script density, and deduplicated via SHA-256 hashes into 195,086 documents. Deterministic shuffling (seed 42) produced train.txt (566.65 MB, 191,184 docs), val.txt (6.20 MB), and test.txt (5.26 MB).

3. Question 1: Early Merges & Letter Reconstruction

1. Question 3: Qualitative Failure Cases Analysis

To identify architectural and data boundaries, we examined three specific inputs where our tokenizer exhibits sub-optimal segmentation, high token inflation, or excessive fragmentation:

Failure Case 1: Colloquial Roman Urdu with Character Elongation
Input: "yrrr plzzzzz mujhy btao ye assignment kb tk submit krwani h wrna scene off hojana" (15 words)
Token Count: Custom Urdu BPE = 42 tokens (2.80 tok/word) | o200k_base = 25 | GPT-2 = 29
Segmentation: yr | r | r | pl | z | z | z | z | z | m | uj | hy...
Linguistic & Algorithmic Explanation: Because the 566 MB training corpus was exclusively curated in Perso-Arabic Nastaliq script, Roman Urdu informal speech with expressive character repetitions (e.g., 'yrrr', 'plzzzzz', 'wrna') represents an out-of-domain distribution. The tokenizer lacks subword merges for Latin letter combinations and is forced to fall back to individual 1-byte ASCII tokens ('p', 'l', 'z', 'z', 'z'), leading to a 2.80 tokens/word inflation.
Failure Case 2: Stacked Multi-Diacritic Combining Sequences (Aerab)
Input: "مُسْتَشْفَیَاتٌ (Mustashfayatun) بِالْإِضَافَةِ إِلَى تَسْخِیرِ الطَّاقَاتِ" (6 words)
Token Count: Custom Urdu BPE = 39 tokens (6.50 tok/word) | o200k_base = 49 | GPT-2 = 77
Segmentation: م | ُس | ْتَ | ش | ْف | َی | َات | ٌ | ( | M...
Linguistic & Algorithmic Explanation: While the regex pattern \p{M} keeps diacritics attached to words, standard modern Urdu digital text is unvocalized. When encountering classical Arabic loanwords adorned with full phonetic diacritics (zabar, zer, pesh, sukun, tanween, and tashdeed simultaneously), the specific character-plus-aerab combinations never met the frequency threshold during BPE training. The model fragments every vowel mark into isolated 2-byte Unicode tokens, causing an inflation of 6.50 tokens/word.
Failure Case 3: Rare Unicode Presentation-Form Ligatures & Honorifics
Input: "نبی کریم ﷺ اور صحابہ کرام ؓ و ؑ کے ارشادات مبارکہ" (12 words)
Token Count: Custom Urdu BPE = 12 tokens | o200k_base = 22 tokens | GPT-2 = 59 tokens
Linguistic & Algorithmic Explanation: Unicode presentation-form glyphs such as Sallallah-o-Alaihi-Wasallam (ﷺ U+FDFA) and Razi-Allahu-Anhu (ؓ U+0618) occupy 3 to 4 bytes in UTF-8. While our model correctly tokenizes frequent standard Urdu words into single units (1.0 tok/word), rarer religious markers or non-standard calligraphy ligatures (e.g. ﷽ U+FDFD, taking 39 bytes) fragment into raw byte primitives, demonstrating that specialized ligatures require explicit corpus balancing.
